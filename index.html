How a landlord is classed as distressed or strained
LevelTest (any one is enough)What it means for buyingEvidence
DistressedAn insolvency process is still running: court winding-up (liquidation), creditors' voluntary liquidation, administration, or a CVA, or a lender has appointed a receiver over its property that has not yet been discharged.The owner no longer controls the sale. A liquidator, administrator or receiver is selling, usually quickly. Call them.Companies House insolvency filings (e.g. COCOMP, LIQ02, AM01, RM01, CVA1)
StrainedNo formal process, but hard evidence of money trouble: a bridging loan still outstanding after 15+ months (these are usually 12-month loans); a loan from a private individual; a second-charge loan; accounts overdue; or an active strike-off notice.The owner still decides, but likely needs to refinance or sell within months. Approach the owner directly.Companies House charges register and filing history
Lot for saleA property in an auction catalogue where the listing says it is sold by receivers (lender-appointed), by the mortgagee (the lender), or by administrators / a liquidator.The asset itself is being sold to repay debt, with a fixed auction date. Bid, or make a pre-auction offer to the auctioneer.Auction listing text (Allsop, Savills, SDL, Auction House)

Not counted: solvent liquidations, processes that have ended, withdrawn strike-off notices, dissolved companies, non-property companies, pension trustees, and weak signals such as a specialist lender alone. Company owners only; individuals need Nimbus.

Before calling an agreement bankable

  1. Confirm the legal signing entity and whether a group/council actually guarantees its rent. A nomination or care contract is not a landlord rent guarantee.
  2. Get written acceptance of location, unit sizes, use, licensing/EPC standards, client cohort and the full 5-30-unit tranche.
  3. Verify agreed base rent, reviews/caps, payment start, void cover, benefit-risk allocation, repairs, insurance and compliance responsibilities.
  4. Read audited cash/liquidity, unrestricted reserves, debt/lease liabilities, regulator judgements and funding concentration, for the signing entity, not only its group.
  5. Match lease duration and break clauses to commissioning/referral arrangements. Identify who pays during gaps and on early termination.
  6. Require solicitor and lender review, security/guarantees where appropriate, stress tests and a viable alternative letting plan.
